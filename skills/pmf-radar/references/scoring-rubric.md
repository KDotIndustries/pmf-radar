# Scoring Rubric

Scores are 1 to 5 judgment aids. Do not blindly average them. Explain the
judgment, which dimensions matter most, and which weak or missing signals
should override a tempting average.

## Verdict Labels

- Build now
- Run paid test
- Research more
- Park
- Kill
- Internal tool only

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pain intensity | Is the pain severe or just annoying? |
| Frequency | Does it happen daily/weekly or rarely? |
| Buyer clarity | Is there an obvious buyer or economic owner? |
| Existing spend | Are people already paying for tools, staff, agencies, or workarounds? |
| Workaround ugliness | Are current solutions manual, fragmented, expensive, or hated? |
| Category validation | Do competitors, service providers, budgets, reviews, pricing, or hiring show that buyers already spend in this category? |
| Wedge validation | Is there evidence that the proposed wedge solves a specific unmet need, not just that the broad category exists? |
| Competitive gap | Are existing tools weak, overpriced, complex, or incomplete? |
| Distribution accessibility | Can the founder reach buyers directly? |
| MVP feasibility | Can a useful version be tested in 1-2 weeks? |
| AI advantage | Does AI change the economics or workflow meaningfully? |
| Trust/compliance risk | Is the idea burdened by regulation, safety, privacy, or trust issues? Reverse-score this. |
| Founder-market fit | Does the founder have relevant experience, access, or insight? |
| Speed to paid test | Can the founder ask for payment, LOI, or pilot quickly? |

## Category Validation vs Wedge Validation

Competitors can prove that a category has buyers, budgets, workflows, and
existing demand. Treat this as category validation, not proof that the user's specific wedge will win.

Ask two separate questions:

1. Is the category validated by real spend, active competitors, customer
   reviews, job posts, agencies, integrations, pricing, or public customer
   stories?
2. Is the wedge validated by evidence that buyers want this specific angle,
   such as a cheaper/faster workflow, native embedding, API-first access,
   vertical specialization, better distribution, compliance control, or a
   clearly hated incumbent gap?

If the category is validated and the wedge can be tested by building a small
sellable artifact faster than by running interviews, Build now can be the right
verdict. In that case, Build now means build the smallest sellable wedge to
test distribution and conversion, not build a full product.

Do not recommend Build now for a broad clone. If competitors solve the broad
outcome well and the wedge is only "same thing but ours," choose Research more,
Park, or Run paid test.

Example: customer service software is a validated category because companies
buy support outcomes from existing tools. "Headless customer service" is a
different wedge. It earns Build now only if evidence shows a buyer who wants to
own the UI or embed support natively, such as B2B SaaS teams building custom
support UIs, white-label platforms, AI app builders needing handoff APIs, or
regulated teams needing backend/control separation. Otherwise, buyers likely
prefer a complete support tool and the wedge is unvalidated.

## Interpretation

- Build now: category is validated, the wedge is specific, the buyer is clear,
  distribution is reachable, and a small sellable prototype is the fastest way
  to test conversion.
- Run paid test: enough evidence to sell before building, but the wedge or
  willingness to pay can be tested without product work.
- Research more: signals are promising but incomplete.
- Park: interesting but not urgent or not reachable.
- Kill: weak pain, unclear buyer, no spend, or impossible wedge.
- Internal tool only: useful for the founder but weak as a standalone business.
